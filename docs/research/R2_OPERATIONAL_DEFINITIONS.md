# R2 — Operational Definitions

This document defines the constructs that later R3 implementation must represent. It intentionally avoids arbitrary numeric mastery cutoffs before expert review and pilot validation.

## 1. Learning Objective

### Conceptual definition

A discrete, assessable learning target representing a competency students are expected to demonstrate within the selected programming topic.

### Operational definition

A versioned objective record with at minimum:

- objective code;
- title/statement;
- course/context;
- sequence/order;
- optional cognitive level/tag;
- active/version status.

Every research material and scored assessment item used as mastery evidence must be explicitly aligned to at least one objective.

### Unit of analysis

Student × Learning Objective.

---

## 2. Material–Objective Alignment

### Conceptual definition

The declared relationship between learning content and the objective(s) that content is intended to teach or support.

### Operational definition

A persisted mapping between a learning material and one or more learning objectives.

The mapping may later include an instructional role such as:

- primary instruction;
- remediation;
- enrichment;
- prerequisite/review.

The role list is a design option for R3; no runtime schema is created in R2.

---

## 3. Question–Objective Alignment

### Conceptual definition

The relationship between an assessment item and the learning objective it is intended to measure.

### Operational definition

A persisted mapping between a scored question and one or more objectives, including an explicit contribution/weight only if the research design justifies multi-objective items.

Preferred research design:

- keep most items aligned to one primary objective;
- avoid ambiguous multi-objective scoring where possible;
- preserve cognitive level/difficulty as metadata, not as a substitute for objective alignment.

---

## 4. Learning Evidence

### Conceptual definition

An observed, timestamped student action or assessment result that may inform interpretation of learning state.

### Evidence classes

#### Primary mastery evidence

Evidence that can directly contribute to objective-level mastery estimation after alignment and validation:

- scored answer to an objective-aligned assessment item;
- objective-aligned reassessment/remedial item result;
- other scored performance evidence only if explicitly validated later.

#### Contextual evidence

Evidence useful for interpreting behavior or intervention delivery but which must **not automatically inflate mastery**:

- material opened/completed;
- time on material;
- number of attempts;
- recommendation viewed;
- remediation opened/completed;
- sequence/navigation events.

### Operational definition

A research event/evidence record that identifies:

- de-identified student/research participant;
- course;
- learning objective when applicable;
- source object (question/material/recommendation/etc.);
- event/evidence type;
- timestamp;
- raw observed value;
- treatment condition where relevant;
- algorithm/configuration version where the event is generated by the adaptive mechanism.

---

## 5. Mastery

### Conceptual definition

An estimated state representing the degree to which a student has demonstrated the targeted learning objective based on valid aligned evidence.

### Operational definition

A student-objective state containing:

- mastery estimate;
- evidence count;
- evidence window/version;
- last evidence time;
- evidence sufficiency status;
- calculation/model version;
- trace/reference to evidence used.

### Important distinction

Mastery is an **estimate**, not an observed fact.

Therefore AI-LCMS must not display a mastery value without making its evidence basis auditable.

### Scale

Implementation may use a normalized scale such as 0–1 or 0–100, but R2 does not define final cutoffs between adaptive states.

---

## 6. Evidence Sufficiency

### Conceptual definition

Whether the quantity and quality of available evidence are adequate for the system to issue a strong adaptive recommendation.

### Operational definition

A separate state from mastery itself, for example:

- `insufficient`;
- `sufficient`.

Potential criteria to validate later may include:

- minimum number of aligned scored observations;
- minimum coverage of the targeted objective;
- absence of unresolved/invalid assessment evidence.

Existing product values such as “minimum 3 responses” are class-level analytics heuristics and must not be silently reused as a student-level evidence-sufficiency rule.

---

## 7. Adaptive Decision

### Conceptual definition

A system decision category produced from validated mastery evidence and evidence-sufficiency rules.

### Proposed operational states

- `insufficient_evidence`;
- `remediation_candidate`;
- `continue_candidate`;
- `enrichment_candidate`.

These names are stable design categories; their numeric thresholds remain research parameters to be established later.

---

## 8. Adaptive Recommendation

### Conceptual definition

An explainable recommendation for the student’s next learning action based on an adaptive decision.

### Operational definition

A persisted recommendation containing at minimum:

- student;
- objective;
- adaptive decision state;
- recommended target/action;
- human-readable rationale;
- mastery snapshot before recommendation;
- evidence sufficiency state;
- algorithm/configuration version;
- generated timestamp;
- exposure timestamp/status;
- final action/outcome linkage.

A recommendation is not considered an intervention merely because it was generated. Exposure must be recorded separately.

---

## 9. Intervention

### Conceptual definition

The learning action actually delivered or made available because of an adaptive recommendation.

### Operational categories

- remediation;
- continue/next objective;
- enrichment/challenge;
- no adaptive action due to insufficient evidence.

### Treatment integrity

The study must distinguish:

- recommendation generated;
- recommendation shown;
- recommendation followed;
- intervention completed.

---

## 10. Remediation

### Conceptual definition

Additional targeted learning support for an objective not yet sufficiently demonstrated.

### Operational definition

A material/activity explicitly linked to the same objective as the evidence that triggered the remediation decision, followed where feasible by objective-aligned reassessment.

---

## 11. Enrichment

### Conceptual definition

A more advanced or extended learning activity offered when evidence indicates the student is ready for additional challenge.

### Operational definition

An enrichment resource/activity linked to the relevant objective or a clearly defined next-level objective.

Enrichment must not be treated as evidence of mastery merely because the student opened it.

---

## 12. Reassessment

### Conceptual definition

A subsequent assessment used to collect new evidence after an intervention.

### Operational definition

A scored assessment event linked to:

- the same objective;
- the preceding recommendation/intervention when applicable;
- a timestamp after the intervention.

This linkage enables before/after analysis without pretending that the intervention caused every observed score change.

---

## 13. Recommendation Rationale

### Conceptual definition

The traceable explanation for why a recommendation was produced.

### Operational definition

A structured and human-readable explanation based on:

- objective;
- mastery snapshot;
- evidence sufficiency;
- policy version;
- evidence summary.

Avoid explanations such as “AI recommends this” without evidence trace.

---

## 14. Teacher Decision / Override

### Conceptual definition

Instructor oversight of a recommendation where the workflow requires human review.

### Operational definition

One of:

- accept;
- modify;
- reject.

The record should include:

- teacher;
- recommendation;
- decision;
- optional/required reason depending on the final protocol;
- timestamp.

This supports auditability and analysis of agreement between system recommendations and teacher judgement.

---

## 15. Adaptive Condition

### Operational definition

The experimental condition in which objective-level evidence is allowed to generate and expose adaptive recommendations/interventions according to the frozen research policy.

---

## 16. Non-Adaptive Condition

### Operational definition

The comparison condition using the same core AI-LCMS platform, content and assessment infrastructure, but without individualized adaptive recommendations driving the student’s next learning action.

Ordinary non-adaptive course progression must be predefined to avoid hidden instructor-driven adaptation that contaminates the comparison.

---

## 17. Primary Learning Outcome

### Conceptual definition

Student achievement on the targeted programming objectives after the learning intervention period.

### Operational definition

A posttest/research assessment score aligned to the same research objectives and analyzed with pretest/baseline performance considered in the statistical model.

The posttest should not simply be the internal mastery score generated by the adaptive mechanism.

---

## 18. Product Quality

### Operational definition

Selected measurable properties of AI-LCMS derived from relevant characteristics in ISO/IEC 25010:2023 and project requirements.

ISO/IEC 25010 is treated as a quality model, not as a single questionnaire or universal percentage score.

---

## 19. Usability / Quality in Use

### Conceptual definition

Usability is considered in a specified context of use, including effectiveness, efficiency and satisfaction.

### Operational measures planned for AI-LCMS

- task completion/success;
- time on task;
- errors or critical failures;
- assistance required where recorded;
- user satisfaction instrument to be selected/validated for the research context;
- qualitative issue notes.

Existing SUS support may remain for historical/formative comparison, but it is not the primary thesis evaluation framework.

---

## 20. Configuration Version

### Operational definition

A reproducibility identifier for the mastery/adaptive policy used to generate research decisions.

Any final research parameters must be frozen and versioned before the main experiment.

Changes after the experiment begins require explicit protocol documentation rather than silent replacement.
